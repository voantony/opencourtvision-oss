# How to film a match for Vamos

1. **Camera position.** Put one phone behind the baseline, centred, on the fence or a clamp, about 2–3 m high. The whole court has to be in frame: both baselines, both sidelines and the net. For padel, film from outside the back glass, centred.
2. **Landscape, 1080p, 60 fps if your phone offers it.** 30 fps works, but fast serves blur. Turn off stabilisation and HDR, and lock the zoom at 1×.
3. **Don't touch it.** No moving, zooming or pausing during the match. If the camera gets bumped, note the game score when it happened.
4. **Light.** Avoid filming straight into the sun. Under floodlights, film before it gets fully dark. Make sure the ball is visible to you on the phone screen.
5. **Battery and storage.** Plug the phone into a power bank. A 2-hour match at 1080p60 takes about 15–25 GB.
6. **Start recording before the warm-up and stop after the last point.** Vamos finds the start of the match itself.
7. **Write down the final score** (and a set score if you remember it). It's how we check Vamos.
8. **Everyone on court agrees, and everyone is 18 or older.** Ask the other players before you film. Never film or upload a match with minors in it.
9. **Bad ideas.** Side-on angles, filming from the bench, handheld, through a dirty or wet fence, or fisheye/0.5× lenses.

What to send as feedback (do not upload footage — it stays on your computer):

- Your written final score, and the score Vamos gave with no taps
- How many taps it took to fix the score
- Any points Vamos missed or invented, and roughly when they happened (game score)
- Any cuts that clipped a serve toss or a last shot
- Court type (hard, clay, artificial grass, padel), indoor or outdoor, phone model, and fps
